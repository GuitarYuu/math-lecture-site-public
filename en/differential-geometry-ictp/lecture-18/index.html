    Chapter: Global Differential Geometry

    Hyperbolic models

    Poincaré disk:

    \[ D = \{(x,y) \mid x^2 + y^2 < 1\} \]
    \[ \mathrm{I} = \frac{4}{\bigl(1 - (x^2 + y^2)\bigr)^2}\,(dx^2 + dy^2) \]

    Upper half plane:

    \[ U = \{(x,y) \mid y > 0\} \]
    \[ \mathrm{I} = \frac{dx^2 + dy^2}{y^2} \]
    \[ \varphi: D \to U,\quad z \mapsto i \frac{1-z}{1+z},\quad \varphi^{-1}(w) = \frac{i-w}{i+w} \]

    Isometries:

    Möbius transformations:

    \(a,b,c,d \in \mathbb{R}\), \(ad - bc > 0\)

    \[ z \mapsto \frac{az + b}{cz + d} = w \]
    \[ w \mapsto \frac{dw - b}{-cw + a} \]

    these are isometries of \(U\)

    the map \(z \to -\bar{z}\) is an isometry.

    I would like to know the geodesics of these models.

    Geodesics in \(U\):

    \[ \begin{cases} \frac{d}{ds}\bigl(E u' + F v'\bigr) = \frac{1}{2}\bigl(E_u u'^2 + 2F_v u'v' + G_u v'^2\bigr) \quad (1) \\ \frac{d}{ds}\bigl(F u' + G v'\bigr) = \frac{1}{2}\bigl(E_v u'^2 + 2F_v u'v' + G_v v'^2\bigr) \quad (2) \end{cases} \]
    \[ G = E = \frac{1}{v^2},\quad F = 0,\quad K = -1 \]
    \[ (1) \iff \frac{d}{ds}\left(\frac{u'}{v^2}\right) = 0 \implies u' = c v^2 \quad \text{for some } c \in \mathbb{R} \]
    \[ \text{p.a.l.}\implies 1 = \frac{u'^2 + v'^2}{v^2} \quad (3) \]
    \[ (1)+(3) \Rightarrow (2) \]
    \[ \begin{cases} u' = c v^2 \\ u'^2 + v'^2 = v^2 \end{cases} \]

    a) \(c = 0 \implies u = \text{constant}\)

    b) \(c \neq 0 \implies \frac{dv}{du} = \sqrt{\frac{v^2 - c^2 v^4}{c^2 v^4}} \implies \frac{c v \, dv}{\sqrt{1 - c^2 v^2}} = du\)

    \[ \implies -\frac{1}{c}\sqrt{1 - c^2 v^2} = u - a \implies (u - a)^2 + v^2 = \frac{1}{c^2} \]

    Differentiable manifold:

    regular surfaces, \(S \subset \mathbb{R}^3\) s.t. \(\forall p \in S\) \(\exists\) neighborhood of \(p\) in \(\mathbb{R}^3\), \(V\), and a map \(f_\alpha: U_\alpha \subset \mathbb{R}^2 \to V \cap S\) s.t.

    1) \(f_\alpha\) is \(C^\infty\) and homeomorphism

    2) \((df_\alpha)_q: T_q U_\alpha \to \mathbb{R}^3\) is injective

    \(\implies\) If \((U_\alpha, f_\alpha), (U_\beta, f_\beta)\) s.t. \(f_\alpha(U_\alpha) \cap f_\beta(U_\beta) = W \neq \varnothing\)

    The two transition maps

    \[ \begin{aligned} f_\beta^{-1} \circ f_\alpha &: f_\alpha^{-1}(W) \longrightarrow f_\beta^{-1}(W), \\ f_\alpha^{-1} \circ f_\beta &: f_\beta^{-1}(W) \longrightarrow f_\alpha^{-1}(W) \end{aligned} \]

    are \(C^\infty\).

    Definition

    An \(n\)-dimensional differentiable manifold is a set \(M\) with a family of injective maps \(f_\alpha: U_\alpha \to M\), \(U_\alpha \subset \mathbb{R}^n\) open, s.t.

    1) \(\bigcup\limits_\alpha f_\alpha(U_\alpha) = M\)

    2) \(\forall \alpha,\beta\) s.t. \(f_\alpha(U_\alpha) \cap f_\beta(U_\beta) = W \neq \varnothing\) , then \(f_\alpha^{-1}(W)\) and \(f_\beta^{-1}(W)\) are open sets in \(\mathbb{R}^n\) and \(f_\beta^{-1} \circ f_\alpha\) and \(f_\alpha^{-1} \circ f_\beta\) are \(C^\infty\).

    3) The atlas \(\{(U_\alpha,f_\alpha)\}\) is maximal relative to 1) and 2).

    Observation: \(\{(U_\alpha,f_\alpha)\}\) (differentiable structure) induces a topology on \(M\): \(A \subset M\) is open if \(f_\alpha^{-1}(A \cap f_\alpha(U_\alpha))\) is open in \(\mathbb{R}^n\) \(\forall \alpha\).

    In general, this topology is not Hausdorff and does not have a countable basis.

    From now on we assume that this topology is Hausdorff and has countable basis.

    Clearly regular surfaces are two-dimensional differentiable manifolds.

    Example

    Projective plane \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) (\(\mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{R})\))

    the set of lines passing through \(0\) in \(\mathbb{R}^3\)

    \[ \mathbb{R}^3 \setminus \{0\} \big/ \sim \quad [x,y,z] \]
    \[ (x,y,z) \sim (\lambda x, \lambda y, \lambda z),\ \lambda \neq 0 \]
    \[ V_1 = \bigl\{ [x,y,z] \in \mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{R}) \mid x \neq 0 \bigr\} \]
    \[ V_2 = \bigl\{ [x,y,z] \in \mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{R}) \mid y \neq 0 \bigr\} \]
    \[ V_3 = \bigl\{ [x,y,z] \in \mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{R}) \mid z \neq 0 \bigr\} \]
    \[ f_i: \mathbb{R}^2 \to V_i,\quad (u,v) \mapsto \begin{cases} [1,u,v], & i=1 \\ [u,1,v], & i=2 \\ [u,v,1], & i=3 \end{cases} \]

    Claim: \(\{(V_i,f_i)\}\) is a differentiable structure (up to 3).

    1. \(V_1 \cup V_2 \cup V_3 = \mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{R})\).

    2. \(\alpha=1,\ \beta=2\) \(f_1^{-1}(V_1 \cap V_2)=\{(u,v) \mid u \neq 0\}\)

    \[ (f_2^{-1} \circ f_1)(u,v) = f_2^{-1}\bigl([1,u,v]\bigr) = f_2^{-1}\left(\left[\frac{1}{u},1,\frac{v}{u}\right]\right) = \left(\frac{1}{u},\frac{v}{u}\right) \in C^\infty \]
